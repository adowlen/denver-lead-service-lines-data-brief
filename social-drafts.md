## X thread (Alex posts; no tag line)

1/ 21,426 premises are still in Denver Water's lead service line replacement program, per its June 30, 2026 inventory. 11,400 are confirmed lead lines. 10,026 more are unknown but considered likely lead.

2/ 18,775 of the remaining premises are in the City and County of Denver - one in ten city premises. The largest counts: Platte Park 1,636, Washington Park 1,265, Berkeley 1,168.

3/ Shares, not just counts: Country Club 68.3% of premises still in the program, Platte Park 61.9%, Rosedale 55.0%. Five neighborhoods are over half. Citywide: 10.0%.

4/ Two different jobs remain. Platte Park's pool is 85% confirmed and waiting on replacement. Country Club's is 84% unconfirmed and waiting on investigation. Same program, different work.

5/ The pool tracks housing age. Premises built in 1950 or earlier hold 77.1% of Denver's remaining pool. Post-1960 premises: a 1 to 2% pool share.

6/ Pace: Denver Water's published replacements layer records an average of 5,609 replacements a year over 2020-2023. Clearing today's pool by the end of 2037 takes an average of 1,863 a year. By Denver Water's own pre-2035 goal: 2,521 a year.

7/ One catch: the public replacements layer ends May 2, 2024. The current rate is not in the public data. The full brief, with every figure verified against the raw inventory: https://github.com/adowlen/denver-lead-service-lines-data-brief
